# Adjudication, owner checkpoints and amendments

## Per-slice procedure

1. Run the quotechecker on every scout report; fix malformed evidence and mismatches before relying on it. Record human verification of every `cmd:` or `$` item, with the actual command context, output and verifier. The checker never executes it. The lead verifies all critical/major claims from source and samples minors; independently check every premise that drives a ruling. Recheck old baseline facts when a later argument depends on them.
2. Consolidate duplicates under stable IDs; retain their original IDs in `seen_in` and source links. Keep evidence, keep/cut lists and rejected routes. Separate claims-only challenger inputs from recommendation/route inputs. Falsifiers see claim, evidence and paths, without recommendation or severity. Recommendation judges see proposals/routes, their necessary context and pillars, without prior verdicts. No challenger sees another's conclusion before reporting.
3. Maintain a coverage matrix with one row per finding and a separate row per recommendation/route. Each row names at least **two independent blind reviewers**; critical or roadmap-changing rows need **three**. Pairing one claim review with one recommendation review is not two reviews of each. Use separate briefs or passes with controlled inputs and bounded waves; preserve blindness even if slots are limited. Ask code falsifiers for their own reproductions. “Holds”, “overstated”, “refuted” and “unsure” are valid results; uncertainty is not a demand to invent a defect.
4. Adjudicate from evidence. Reword overstatements, rerate severity and explicitly resolve disagreements. Critical means that failing to decide now makes about-to-ship work take the wrong shape. When timing alone differs, consider a cheap seam now and the full feature with its first consumer, without treating that as an accepted decision before the owner checkpoint. Flag contradictions with archived SHALL requirements for a MODIFIED spec delta. Claims about unseen register material may be “unsure” for blind reviewers; the lead verifies the register directly.
5. Write the slice digest and append the register before presenting it. Preserve claim-kind tags, challenge coverage and every disposition. Status progression is candidate → verified → challenged → accepted/accepted-partial/downgraded/rejected/parked; preserve owner-deferred and blocked outcomes explicitly. Retain source quotations, reproduction references, recommendation decisions, rationale, fixing owner or revisit trigger and later delivery evidence. Update the review's README status and its parent review index if present; create a concise local index when needed for navigation.
6. Give the owner a blunt verdict, top five findings, finding table, alternatives/unconsidered routes, keep/cut list and 2–4 concrete decisions (usually the top three). Checkpoint after slice 0; hard stop after slice 1; checkpoint after each of 2–6. Record the owner's exact answers as pillars or per-finding decisions **before the next slice**. Draft amendments together at synthesis, not opportunistically during scouting. A later request to continue resumes this register; it does not erase parked/rejected decisions.

## Slice 7: binding synthesis sequence

1. **Revise:** two scouts revise the ordered change plan and amendment inventory from the register and earlier audits. Every accepted item has one fixing owner by actual scope, not merely an ID mention. Keep untouched planned work and its dependencies. An amendment has ID, target, exact anchor quote, accepted wording/action and one content batch. Script-check duplicates, dependency references/order, ownership coverage, quote anchors and batch membership; retain output.
2. **Four-auditor re-audit**, all independent and each brief **high reasoning, medium-to-high model**:
   - A1: plan coverage, accepted order and fidelity against scope text.
   - A2: granularity, size rule, practical sequencing, milestones and merges/splits without reducing gates.
   - A3: inventory fidelity/coverage; distinguish owner questions, settled decisions and additions nobody accepted.
   - A4: simulate apply batches, detect anchor conflicts, budgets, cross-artifact inconsistencies and cross-slice contradictions.
3. **Written rulings:** independently verify the four reports and their load-bearing premises. Write one ruling file resolving conflicts before either reviser proceeds. Keep actual owner questions for the checkpoint; do not decide them by auditor vote.
4. **Revise again:** revisers apply those written rulings. Each runs self-checks; the lead reruns them independently. A reviser's “nothing missing” is not coverage evidence: inspect scope content against each accepted finding and the owner's exact wording.
5. **Two-auditor final gate:** one independent auditor checks fidelity, ownership and coverage/order; the other checks application readiness, coherence, anchors and conflicts. Both receive the resulting artifacts, register and rulings. Both briefs state **high reasoning, medium-to-high model**. Verify findings. Material unresolved questions return to the owner; final editorial/apply constraints become a **binding punch list**, avoiding an endless cosmetic revision loop.
6. Present the plan, inventory, rulings and punch list for owner approval. Later roadmap directions remain revisable. Propose only the **next roadmap change**, not a group of future proposals. Keep subsequent work as roadmap entries.

## Approved amendments, then a separate restructure

Design-document amendments may be applied directly as approved review output. Code, tests, skills and shipped-code AGENTS rewrites go through OpenSpec delivery. Neither the review nor its recommendation authorizes implementation or commits without the applicable owner checkpoint. Use the default checkout and preserve unrelated work.

For each approved content batch: give one applier its exact amendment IDs, the register, written rulings and binding punch list. Follow [document integrity](../../uplift-review-gates/references/document-integrity.md), match current verbatim anchors, apply only that batch, and report applied/skipped IDs plus every interpretation. Run `npm run verify`, then obtain independent fidelity and coherence reviews (**high reasoning, medium-to-high model**). Fidelity compares the diff to the accepted inputs and protects untargeted content. Coherence reads the resulting docs, statuses, links and dependencies. Verify and fix findings, rerun checks, present the concrete diff and commit only after the owner's applicable approval.

After approved content batches, do a **separate restructure** by system. First produce an old-paragraph/block → new-home/merge/superseded-decision traceability map. Preserve every concept, number, identifier, owner sentence and exemplar. Obtain independent no-loss and coherence reviews before cutting or replacing content. Keep one canonical statement and pointers; maintain status marks, indexes, roadmap and dispositions. Fix verified findings, rerun verification and preserve the approval checkpoint. Do not combine “is this accepted content?” and “where should it live?” into an unaudited rewrite.

Finish with the retained register, delivery mapping, unresolved owner questions, parked/rejected decisions and exact next action. Future periodic reviews start by reconciling that record against what actually shipped.
